The Place

A Fortress of Olives

Zgharta. The name comes from the Aramaic for “fortress.” It sits on a sun-drenched plain in North Lebanon, cradled between two rivers, eleven kilometres from the sea.

This is one of the oldest olive-growing regions on earth. The Phoenicians pressed Oil on this coast and carried it across the Mediterranean in round-bottomed ships—to Greece, to Italy, to Spain. They gave the world the olive tree. The groves on the Plain of Zgharta are a continuation of that ancient, unbroken line.

Our family has tended the same trees here for three generations. The soil is mineral-rich, shaped by the Jouit and Rashein rivers. The summers are long and golden. The olives grow slowly, absorbing everything the land has to give. You can taste it.

The Process

Picked and Pressed on the Same Day.

Nothing Between the Fruit and the Bottle.

We harvest early—when the olives are still green and firm, and the polyphenol content is at its peak. Every olive is picked by hand. No rakes, no machines. The fruit goes straight to the press within hours of leaving the tree.

The result is an Oil that’s green and vibrant, peppery at the back of the throat, with a clean, grassy brightness that fades into something almost sweet. No blending. No refining. No additives. Just the pure, undiluted expression of this place.
